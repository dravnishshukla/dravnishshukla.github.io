Most of us spend our lives in rapidly growing urban environments, yet we are rarely able to visualize the chronic influence of pollutants in the air we breathe and the ubiquitous noise around us, which shape our daily lifestyle and health. As an environmental health researcher, my goal is to make those invisible threats measurable and actionable. Currently, as a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Bombay, I am investigating the vertical dynamics of urban air and noise pollution. By combining strategic field monitoring with indoor-outdoor exposure assessments, I map how particulate matter (PM2.5), aerosol toxicity, and noise frequencies vary across high-rise residential buildings. My focus is on understanding exactly what we are exposed to, regardless of which floor we live on. 
My background is based on assessing complex environmental exposures in highly vulnerable settings. During my Ph.D. in Environmental Engineering at the S.V. National Institute of Technology, I led an extensive field-based investigation into the dynamic, high-risk environments of roadside schools on the front lines of heavy urban traffic. This required significant logistical and analytical challenges, including evaluating over 1,500 students and conducting clinical assessments on teachers. Through this, I mapped exactly how chronic frontline exposure disrupts cognitive development and degrades daily well-being in the classroom. For me, environmental engineering is not just about assessment; it is about ensuring that public health remains the non-negotiable priority at the center of my research.
